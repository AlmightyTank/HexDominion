## Tactical orders

`UnitOrders.tactical()` issues the three persistent orders. All of them accept a multi-unit selection and each unit is validated independently, so a mixed army gets the order only where the movement domain and route allow it.

| Order | Key | Behaviour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Move | Right-click | Existing pathfinding rules, terrain costs, roads and domain restrictions |
| Attack | X / right-click enemy | Explicit target, unchanged pursuit rules |
| Attack-Move | Z | Travels to a destination, stops to fire at any threat in range, commits the edge it is already crossing, then resumes to the destination and ends on arrival |
| Hold Position | J | Fires in range, never pursues across the map |
| Guard | V | Follows a selected friendly unit or defends a chosen hex, leashing two hexes, and ends safely if the guarded unit dies |
| Patrol | O | Loops between 2 and 16 waypoints and engages threats that come into range |

Details that matter for correctness:

- **No teleporting.** A unit that is already crossing a hex edge when it spots a threat finishes that edge before it stops, and the stored destination survives the pause.
- **Guard does not chase.** Guarded units never leave the leash to pursue an enemy, and a dead or removed guard target cancels the order instead of leaving an orphan order running.
- **Fighters keep their mission model.** A fighter rejects geographic Attack-Move, Guard and Patrol outright; it flies explicit strike / return / rearm missions from its airfield and cannot be used as a marching fire platform.
- **Domain rules still apply.** Ships only take water waypoints, aircraft only use their mission system, and ground units cannot patrol into ocean.
- Hold and any new movement order clear the previous persistent order, so a unit never runs two orders at once.

`UnitState` stores the order in `tactical_order` and the guarded unit in a `guard_target` weak reference. Both are saved, validated and restored, so a match reloaded mid-patrol resumes the same patrol and a reloaded guard still follows the same unit.

## Zoom-tier army presentation

`UnitLayer` exposes two configurable thresholds: `far_zoom_threshold` (0.65) and `close_zoom_threshold` (1.5).

| Tier | Zoom | Presentation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| World strategy view | below 0.65 | One compact role marker per hex, faction and unit type with the true unit count, selection ring and damaged state |
| Army view | 0.65 to 1.5 | Small grouped sprites per role with the true unit count, plus the health bar |
| Tactical view | 1.5 and above | Individual unit sprites with facing, health, selection and attack feedback |

Grouping is **presentation only**. `ArmyPresentation` is a single linear pass over the roster that produces batches and per-unit draw offsets; it is shared by drawing and hit testing, so what is clicked is what is drawn. It never merges, duplicates, destroys or otherwise changes a simulated unit: a 520-unit rebuild yields exactly 26 role/faction batches and 520 independent members with unchanged counts.

Hexes with more than one unit of the same role are spread over a ring so the sprites do not overlap; the close tier draws each member at its own offset with its own heading.

## Production and rally points

Factory selection and independent per-facility rally points are unchanged. A rally may now be set to **move** or **attack-move**: the "Rally: attack-move" checkbox in the orders panel applies to the next rally point you set, and newly produced units receive the order through `UnitOrders.tactical`. Invalid destinations are rejected exactly as before, and the existing per-facility queue and rally storage is untouched. Aircraft and ships keep their own movement rules, so a fighter rally still resolves as a strike mission and a ship rally still requires open water.

## Combined-arms enemy AI

`CombinedArmsAI` runs on the existing one-second AI clock, so it costs nothing on render frames.

- **Infantry captures**, vehicles break defended lines, artillery is bought when the enemy holds fortifications, and mobile anti-air is bought when the player has aircraft in the field.
- **Aircraft are limited.** One helicopter and one fighter at most, and only once the ground army already exists, so the AI does not spend its whole budget on air power.
- **Ships and transports are bought when the objective is unreachable by land.** A landing transport is loaded with infantry, sails to the shoreline and unloads on the enemy coast.
- **Defence.** The AI keeps existing defensive and construction priorities, which cover its headquarters, cities, resources and roads, and it stops at the same army cap as before.
- **Efficiency.** The combined-arms step runs after the economy priorities and only spends credits it can afford, so the AI cannot overdraw and cannot starve its power or construction plans.

## Controls

| Action | Desktop | Touch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Attack-Move | Z, then click the destination | Attack-Move button, then tap the destination |
| Guard | V, then click a friendly unit or hex | Guard button, then tap a friendly unit or hex |
| Patrol | O, then click the next waypoint | Patrol button, then tap the next waypoint |
| Attack | X, then click the target | Attack button, then tap the target |
| Hold | J | Hold button |
| Rally as attack-move | Checkbox in the orders panel | Same checkbox |

The orders panel also lists the current order of every selected unit. Escape cancels a pending order without issuing one, and pending orders never survive a menu opening. Zoom and pan gestures are unchanged, so panning cannot accidentally place an order.

## Save format

The existing versioned format gained two unit fields (`tactical_order`, `guard_target`) and one economy field (`rally_order`). Validation rejects unknown order modes, invalid waypoint counts, out-of-map or wrong-domain destinations, guard references to another faction or to the unit itself, and invalid rally order values, all before any live state is changed. Older saves without these fields keep their previous behaviour.

## Verification

`tests/combined_arms_tests.gd` is a new 120-check suite covering the roster, all three new orders and their edge cases, save/load round trips, zoom-tier selection, the large-army grouping pass and the AI purchase and strike decisions. It runs in GitHub validation next to the existing suites.

Local results on Godot 4.7.2 headless, in a clean checkout of this branch without unrelated HUD work:

- 1280 smoke and simulation checks passed
- 120 combined-arms checks passed
- 158 vehicle checks passed
- 91 aircraft checks passed
- 116 naval checks passed
- 73 artillery checks passed
- 784 unit artwork checks passed
- HUD regressions passed, production regressions passed
- Headless import and a 10 second startup check completed without errors

The 520-unit grouping rebuild measured 4317 to 5112 microseconds across two local headless runs. That is a one-off measurement on one machine, not a frame-rate guarantee.

## Limitations

These are real gaps, not formatting placeholders:

- No human has yet played through the new orders on a physical touchscreen, and the small-screen readability of the orders panel has not been verified by eye.
- The new zoom tiers have not been visually reviewed by a human at each threshold; the automated tests check counts, batches and selection anchors rather than how the result looks.
- The AI still advances as a single ground thrust: units walk at the nearest enemy unit, and when there is none they reinforce the weakest border chosen by `DefenseAI`. It does not yet plan coordinated multi-prong invasions, and its naval staging is limited to one transport, one destroyer and one patrol boat.
- The 520-unit measurement is a local rebuild timing, not a frame-rate profile on target hardware.
- The full accessibility and contrast review, balance pass and the afterward antislop audit for this slice are still pending.
